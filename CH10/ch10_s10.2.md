## Section 10.2 — Engineering Economics (AALL1002)

## 📖 1. Introduction
Engineering economics involves the systematic evaluation of the economic merits of proposed solutions to engineering problems. It is vital for engineers to ensure projects are not only technically feasible but also economically viable. The NEC exam heavily tests concepts of time value of money, evaluation of alternatives, and taxation/depreciation systems in Nepal.

## 💡 2. Basic Concept
The fundamental principle of engineering economics is that a dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow due to its earning potential.

> [!NOTE] Definition
> **Time Value of Money**: The concept that money available at the present time is worth more than the same amount in the future due to its potential earning capacity.

## 3. Project Cash Flow
A cash flow diagram is a graphical representation of cash inflows (receipts) and outflows (disbursements) over a project's life.
- **Upward Arrows ($\uparrow$)**: Cash inflows (Revenues, Salvage value).
- **Downward Arrows ($\downarrow$)**: Cash outflows (Initial cost, O&M costs).
- **Horizontal Line**: Time scale, usually in years.

## 4. Discount Rate, Interest, and Time Value of Money
### Interest Rates
- **Simple Interest**: Interest is calculated only on the principal amount.

$$ I = P \times i \times n $$
- **Compound Interest**: Interest is calculated on the principal and the accumulated interest of previous periods.

$$ F = P(1 + i)^n $$
  Where $F$ = Future Value, $P$ = Present Value, $i$ = interest rate per period, $n$ = number of periods.

### Effective vs. Nominal Interest Rate
If interest is compounded $m$ times per year at a nominal rate $r$:
$$ i_{eff} = \left(1 + \frac{r}{m}\right)^m - 1 $$

## ⚙️ 5. Basic Methodologies for Economic Analysis
### 5.1 Minimum Attractive Rate of Return (MARR)
MARR is the minimum acceptable rate of return on an investment. It is the benchmark used to accept or reject project alternatives.

### 5.2 Present Worth Method (NPV)
Net Present Value (NPV) evaluates the present worth of all cash inflows and outflows.
$$ NPV = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{C_t}{(1 + i)^t} $$
- If $NPV > 0$, accept the project.
- If comparing alternatives, choose the one with the highest NPV.

### 5.3 Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
IRR is the discount rate $i^*$ at which NPV equals zero.
$$ \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{C_t}{(1 + i^*)^t} = 0 $$
- If $IRR \ge MARR$, accept the project.

### 5.4 Discounted Payback Period
The time required for cumulative discounted cash flows to equal the initial investment. Unlike simple payback, it accounts for the time value of money.

**Worked Example 1: NPV Calculation**
An engineering firm invests Rs. $10,000$ today, which yields Rs. $3,000$ annually for $5$ years. If MARR is $10\%$, find the NPV.
**Solution:**
$$ NPV = -10000 + 3000(P/A, 10\%, 5) $$
Using formula $(P/A, i, n) = \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i(1+i)^n}$:
$$ (P/A, 10\%, 5) = \frac{1.1^5 - 1}{0.1(1.1^5)} \approx 3.7908 $$
$$ NPV = -10000 + 3000(3.7908) = -10000 + 11372.4 = \text{Rs. } 1372.4 $$
Since $NPV > 0$, the project is acceptable.

## ⚖️ 6. Comparison of Alternatives
When comparing mutually exclusive alternatives, use equivalent methods:
- **Equal Useful Lives**: Compare directly using NPV or Annual Worth (AW).
- **Unequal Useful Lives**: Use the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of lives, or use the AW method, which implicitly assumes repeatability.

## 7. Depreciation and Taxation System in Nepal
### Depreciation
Depreciation is the allocation of the cost of a tangible asset over its useful life.
1. **Straight Line Method (SLM)**:

$$ D = \frac{P - S}{N} $$
   Where $D$ = Annual depreciation, $P$ = Initial cost, $S$ = Salvage value, $N$ = Useful life.
2. **Declining Balance Method (DBM)**:
   A constant percentage is applied to the book value. (Common in Nepal tax systems under Income Tax Act).
   
### Taxation in Nepal
Corporate tax in Nepal applies to net taxable income (Gross Income - Operating Expenses - Depreciation). Taxes act as cash outflows and must be factored into after-tax cash flow analysis. Note that the Income Tax Act categorizes assets into pools (Pool A, B, C, D) with varying depreciation rates (e.g., $5\%, 25\%, 20\%, 15\%$ respectively) using DBM.

## 💡 Common Mistakes / Exam Tips

> [!WARNING]
> Do not confuse nominal and effective interest rates. If a problem states "$12\%$ compounded monthly," you MUST use $1\%$ per month for monthly cash flows, or compute the effective annual rate for annual cash flows.

> [!TIP]
> AW (Annual Worth) is the best method for comparing alternatives with unequal lives, as it avoids computing LCM.

## 📝 Quick Reference / Formula Summary

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - Compound Amount Factor (F/P, i, n): $F = P(1+i)^n$
> - Uniform Series Present Worth (P/A, i, n): $P = A\left[\frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i(1+i)^n}\right]$
> - IRR rule: Accept if $IRR \ge MARR$
