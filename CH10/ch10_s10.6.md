## Section Engineering Regulatory Body (AALL1006)

## 📖 1. Introduction
The regulation of the engineering profession is essential to protect public safety and ensure high standards of practice. In Nepal, this regulatory role is fulfilled by the Nepal Engineering Council (NEC). This section covers the legal framework, registration processes, and ethical codes enforced by the NEC.

## 2. Nepal Engineering Council (Acts & Regulations)
The Nepal Engineering Council (NEC) is the statutory body constituted under the **Nepal Engineering Council Act, 2055 (1999)**.

> [!NOTE] Definition
> **Nepal Engineering Council (NEC)**: The autonomous and corporate body established to make the engineering profession effective, systematic, and to register engineers.

### 2.1. Objectives of NEC Act 2055
- To make the engineering profession effective and systematic.
- To manage the registration of engineers according to their qualifications.
- To determine the standard of engineering education.
- To protect the rights of the public by ensuring engineering services are provided by qualified professionals.

### 2.2. Constitution of the Council
The Council consists of members including:
- A Chairman (appointed by the Government of Nepal).
- A Vice-Chairman.
- Registrar (acts as Member-Secretary).
- Members representing various engineering disciplines and institutions (e.g., IOE, NEA).

## 3. Engineering Council Rules (Regulations)
The Nepal Engineering Council Rules, 2057 (2000) (and subsequent amendments) provide the detailed operational procedures for implementing the Act, including the formats for application, registration fees, and code of conduct details.

## 4. Registration Process and Categories
Registration with the NEC is mandatory to practice engineering in Nepal or to use the title "Engineer".

### 4.1. Categories of Registration
As per the recent amendments and regulations, the NEC has defined specific categories of registration:
1.  **General Registered Engineer**: A graduate with a bachelor's degree in engineering from an institution recognized by NEC. (Usually "Er." title).
2.  **Professional Engineer (PEng)**: An engineer with a master's degree and significant professional experience, who has passed the required evaluation/examination for professional standing.
3.  **Non-Nepalese Registered Engineer**: Foreign engineers working in Nepal on specific projects for a specified duration. Must hold equivalent registration in their home country.

### 4.2. The Registration Process (License Exam)
- **Application**: Submit an application with verified academic transcripts, citizenship certificate, and prescribed fee.
- **Accreditation Check**: The degree must be from a university/college accredited and recognized by the NEC.
- **License Examination**: As per recent amendments (starting around 2023), graduates must pass the NEC License Examination to obtain their registration.
- **Issuance of Certificate**: Upon passing, the applicant's name is entered into the Register, and a Registration Certificate with a unique NEC number is issued.

## 5. Code of Ethics of NEC
The NEC enforces a strict Code of Conduct for registered engineers to ensure professional integrity.

### 5.1. Key Principles of the NEC Code of Ethics
1.  **Discipline and Honesty**: Perform duties with utmost honesty, discipline, and high moral standards.
2.  **Professional Competence**: Undertake assignments only in areas of competence.
3.  **Personal Responsibility**: Take personal responsibility for professional work.
4.  **Impartiality**: Provide impartial and objective advice/decisions.
5.  **Conflict of Interest**: Avoid situations where personal interest conflicts with professional duty. Disclose unavoidable conflicts.
6.  **Confidentiality**: Do not disclose confidential information without consent, except when required by law or to protect public safety.
7.  **Respect for Profession**: Do not maliciously injure the professional reputation of another engineer. Promote the dignity of the profession.

## 6. Disciplinary Actions
The NEC has the authority to take disciplinary action against registered engineers who violate the Code of Conduct or the NEC Act.

### 6.1. Types of Actions
- **Reprimand/Warning**: A formal warning for minor infractions.
- **Suspension**: Temporary removal from the Register for a specified period. The engineer cannot practice during this time.
- **Cancellation of Registration**: Permanent removal of the engineer's name from the Register for severe offenses (e.g., fraud, gross negligence resulting in loss of life/property, conviction of a criminal offense involving moral turpitude).

### 6.2. Investigation Process
- A complaint is filed against an engineer.
- An Investigating Committee is formed by the Council.
- The engineer is given a reasonable opportunity to defend themselves.
- The Council decides on the penalty based on the committee's report.

## 7. Role of NEC in Regulating the Engineering Profession
- **Accreditation**: Assessing and recognizing engineering colleges and their curricula within Nepal and abroad.
- **Standardization**: Setting minimum standards for engineering education.
- **Record Keeping**: Maintaining the updated national Register of Engineers.
- **Enforcement**: Regulating practice through licensing and enforcing the code of conduct.

## 💡 Common Mistakes / Exam Tips

> [!WARNING]
> It is illegal to practice engineering or use the title "Engineer" in Nepal without active registration in the NEC.

> [!TIP]
> Remember the dates: NEC Act was established in 2055 BS, and Rules in 2057 BS. Be clear on the categories of registration, especially the distinction between a General Registered Engineer and a Professional Engineer.

## 📝 Quick Reference / Formula Summary

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **NEC Act**: 2055 BS (1999 AD).
> - **NEC Rules**: 2057 BS (2000 AD).
> - **Categories**: General Registered Engineer, Professional Engineer, Non-Nepalese Registered Engineer.
> - **License Exam**: Now mandatory for general registration.
> - **Penalties**: Warning, Suspension, Cancellation.

## ✏️ Practice Problems
1.  **In which year was the Nepal Engineering Council Act promulgated?**
    a) 2055 BS
    b) 2057 BS
    c) 2019 BS
    d) 2063 BS
    *Answer:* a) 2055 BS.

2.  **Which of the following actions can the NEC take against an engineer found guilty of gross professional misconduct?**
    a) Imprisonment
    b) Cancellation of registration
    c) Confiscation of property
    d) Deportation
    *Answer:* b) Cancellation of registration.

3.  **According to the NEC regulations, an engineer must NOT:**
    a) Undertake continuous professional development.
    b) Disclose a conflict of interest to their client.
    c) Accept financial compensation from more than one party for the same project without consent.
    d) Refuse a project outside their area of expertise.
    *Answer:* c) Accept financial compensation from more than one party for the same project without consent.
