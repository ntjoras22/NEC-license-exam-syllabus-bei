## Section 10.1 — Engineering Drawings and Its Concepts (AALL1001)

## 📖 1. Introduction
Engineering drawing is often referred to as the universal language of engineers. It is a graphical language used to express and record ideas and information necessary for the building of machines, structures, and systems. For the NEC license exam, understanding the fundamental conventions, projections, and standards of engineering drawings is crucial, as it forms the basis for reading and creating technical documents across all engineering disciplines.

## 💡 2. Basic Concept
The core of engineering drawing lies in standardizing how 3D objects are represented on 2D media. Think of it like translating a physical object into a detailed map that anyone trained in the language can read and recreate the exact object.

> [!NOTE] Definition
> **Engineering Drawing**: A type of technical drawing used to fully and clearly define requirements for engineered items. It specifies the shape, dimensions, materials, and other details of the object to be manufactured.

## 3. Fundamentals of Standard Drawing Sheets
Standard drawing sheet sizes are defined by international standards (ISO). The basic principle is that the area of the basic size ($A0$) is $1 \text{ m}^2$, and the sides are in the ratio of $1:\sqrt{2}$.

### ISO A-Series Paper Sizes
When a sheet is folded in half parallel to its shortest side, the two halves have the same aspect ratio as the original.
- **$A0$**: $841 \text{ mm} \times 1189 \text{ mm}$ (Area $\approx 1 \text{ m}^2$)
- **$A1$**: $594 \text{ mm} \times 841 \text{ mm}$
- **$A2$**: $420 \text{ mm} \times 594 \text{ mm}$
- **$A3$**: $297 \text{ mm} \times 420 \text{ mm}$
- **$A4$**: $210 \text{ mm} \times 297 \text{ mm}$

Formula for side ratios:
$$ \frac{y}{x} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414 $$
Where $x$ is the width and $y$ is the length.

## 4. Dimensions and Scale
### Dimensioning
Dimensioning is the process of specifying the size and location of features on a drawing. 
- **Size Dimensions**: Define the overall size of the object (length, width, height, diameter).
- **Location Dimensions**: Define the position of features relative to a reference datum.

### Scale
Scale is the ratio of the linear dimension of an element of an object as represented in the original drawing to the real linear dimension of the same element of the object itself.

> [!NOTE] Definition
> **Scale**: The ratio of drawing size to actual size.

$$ \text{Scale} = \frac{\text{Drawing Size}}{\text{Actual Size}} $$

**Types of Scales:**
1. **Full Size Scale**: $1:1$
2. **Enlarging Scale**: $2:1$, $5:1$, $10:1$ (Used for small components like watch parts)
3. **Reducing Scale**: $1:2$, $1:5$, $1:10$, $1:100$ (Used for large objects like buildings)

**Worked Example 1: Scale Calculation**
A straight road of $50 \text{ km}$ length is represented on a map by a line $10 \text{ cm}$ long. Calculate the Representative Fraction (R.F.) of the scale.
**Solution:**
$$ \text{R.F.} = \frac{\text{Distance on drawing}}{\text{Actual distance}} $$
$$ \text{R.F.} = \frac{10 \text{ cm}}{50 \text{ km}} = \frac{10 \text{ cm}}{50 \times 10^5 \text{ cm}} = \frac{1}{500,000} $$
Scale is $1:500,000$.

## 5. Line Diagram
Line diagrams (or schematic diagrams) represent the physical connections and general layout of an electrical system or pipeline without showing the exact physical dimensions or realistic appearance of the components. They use standard symbols to represent components.

## 6. Projections: Orthographic and Isometric
Projection is a method of mapping a 3D object onto a 2D plane.

### Orthographic Projection
Orthographic projection represents a 3D object using multiple 2D views from different sides (typically Front, Top, and Side views). The projection lines are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the projection plane.

There are two primary systems:
- **First Angle Projection**: The object is placed in the first quadrant. The object lies between the observer and the plane of projection. (Commonly used in Nepal/Europe)
- **Third Angle Projection**: The object is placed in the third quadrant. The plane of projection lies between the observer and the object. (Commonly used in the US)

| Feature | First Angle Projection | Third Angle Projection |
|---------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Object Position | Between observer and projection plane | Behind the projection plane |
| Top View | Below Front View | Above Front View |
| Right Side View | Left of Front View | Right of Front View |

### Isometric Projection
Isometric projection is a method of visually representing 3D objects in 2D in technical and engineering drawings. It is an axonometric projection where the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened, and the angles between any two are 120 degrees.

> [!NOTE] Definition
> **Isometric Scale**: The true length of an object is foreshortened to approx $81.6\%$ of its actual size in isometric projection.

$$ \text{Isometric Length} = \text{True Length} \times \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}} \approx \text{True Length} \times 0.816 $$

**Difference between Isometric View and Isometric Projection:**
- **Isometric View**: Drawn using true scale.
- **Isometric Projection**: Drawn using isometric scale.

## 7. Pictorial Views and Sectional Drawing
### Pictorial Views
Pictorial views show an object as it appears to the human eye. Isometric, oblique, and perspective projections are types of pictorial views.

### Sectional Drawing
Sectional views show the hidden interior features of an object by cutting it with an imaginary cutting plane.
- **Full Section**: The cutting plane passes entirely through the object.
- **Half Section**: The cutting plane passes halfway through the object, removing one quarter of it (useful for symmetrical objects).
- **Offset Section**: The cutting plane is staggered to pass through important features not in a single straight line.
- **Hatching lines**: Drawn at $45^\circ$ to represent solid material cut by the plane.

## 💡 Common Mistakes / Exam Tips

> [!WARNING]
> Students often confuse First Angle and Third Angle placement of views. Remember: in First Angle, the view from a direction is drawn on the *opposite* side. In Third Angle, it is drawn on the *same* side.

> [!WARNING]
> Forgetting to apply the $0.816$ factor when asked specifically for an "Isometric Projection" (rather than an "Isometric View").

> [!TIP]
> In multiple-choice questions regarding scale ratios, verify the units before computing. $1\text{m}$ vs $1\text{mm}$ error is common.

## 📝 Quick Reference / Formula Summary

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - ISO Sheet ratio: width / length = $1 / \sqrt{2}$
> - Isometric length factor = $\sqrt{2/3} \approx 0.816$
> - R.F. (Representative Fraction) = $\frac{\text{Drawing Length}}{\text{Actual Length}}$

## ✏️ Practice Problems
1. The area of an A0 drawing sheet is approximately:
   a) $0.5 \text{ m}^2$
   b) $1.0 \text{ m}^2$
   c) $1.5 \text{ m}^2$
   d) $2.0 \text{ m}^2$
   *Answer Sketch: b. By definition, an A0 sheet has an area of $1 \text{ m}^2$.*

2. In first angle projection, the top view is placed:
   a) Above the front view
   b) Below the front view
   c) To the right of the front view
   d) To the left of the front view
   *Answer Sketch: b. In first angle projection, the object is placed between the observer and the plane, pushing the top view to the horizontal plane below the object.*

3. What is the foreshortening factor for an isometric scale?
   a) $0.707$
   b) $0.816$
   c) $0.866$
   d) $1.000$
   *Answer Sketch: b. The factor is $\sqrt{2/3} \approx 0.816$.*
