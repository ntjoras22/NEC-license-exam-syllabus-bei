## Section Data Communication and Information Theory (AEiE0604)

## 📖 1. Introduction
Data communication and information theory form the backbone of modern digital communication systems. For the NEC exam, understanding how analog signals are converted to digital form, how information is measured, and how data is reliably transmitted over noisy channels is crucial.

## 2. Process of Analog to Digital Communication System
The conversion of an analog signal to a digital format involves three main steps: Sampling, Quantization, and Encoding.

> [!NOTE] Definition
> **Analog to Digital Conversion**: The process of translating continuous-time, continuous-amplitude signals into discrete-time, discrete-amplitude digital signals.

### Block Diagram of A/D Conversion
1. **Sampler**: Converts continuous-time signal $x(t)$ to discrete-time signal $x(nT_s)$.
2. **Quantizer**: Maps the continuous amplitude of each sample into a discrete set of levels.
3. **Encoder**: Assigns a unique binary code to each quantization level.

## 3. Sampling and Pulse Modulation Techniques
Sampling theorem states that a bandlimited signal $x(t)$ with maximum frequency $f_m$ can be perfectly reconstructed from its samples if the sampling rate $f_s$ is:
$$ f_s \geq 2f_m $$

**Pulse Modulation Types:**
- **PAM (Pulse Amplitude Modulation)**: Amplitude of pulse varies with signal amplitude.
- **PWM (Pulse Width Modulation)**: Width of pulse varies with signal amplitude.
- **PPM (Pulse Position Modulation)**: Position of pulse varies with signal amplitude.
- **PCM (Pulse Code Modulation)**: Analog signal is sampled, quantized, and encoded into binary digits.

## 4. Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) and Quantization
PCM is a standard method to digitally represent analog signals.

### Types of Quantization
1. **Uniform Quantization**: Step size $\Delta$ is constant.
2. **Non-uniform Quantization**: Step size varies. Smaller step sizes for smaller amplitudes (companding). e.g., $\mu$-law and $A$-law.

### Quantization Noise
The error introduced during quantization is $q(t) = x_q(t) - x(t)$. For uniform quantization with step size $\Delta$, the quantization error is uniformly distributed between $-\Delta/2$ and $\Delta/2$.

The quantization noise power is:
$$ P_q = \frac{\Delta^2}{12} $$

If the signal has peak-to-peak amplitude $2V_{peak}$ and we use $n$ bits/sample, the number of levels $L = 2^n$. The step size is $\Delta = \frac{2V_{peak}}{L}$.

Signal-to-Quantization Noise Ratio (SQNR) for a full-scale sinusoidal signal:
$$ SQNR_{dB} = 1.76 + 6.02n \text{ dB} $$

## 5. Information Theory and Measure of Information

> [!NOTE] Definition
> **Information ($I$)**: The amount of surprise or uncertainty resolved by the occurrence of an event. For a message with probability $p$, $I = \log_2(1/p)$ bits.

### Entropy ($H$)
The average information per source symbol.
$$ H(X) = - \sum_{i=1}^n p(x_i) \log_2 p(x_i) \text{ bits/symbol} $$
Maximum entropy occurs when all symbols are equiprobable ($H_{max} = \log_2 n$).

### Source Coding
Process of encoding information to reduce redundancy.
- **Shannon-Fano Coding**
- **Huffman Coding**
Average code word length $L = \sum p(x_i) n_i$. Coding efficiency $\eta = H(X) / L$.

## 🎓 6. Shannon-Hartley Channel Capacity Theorem
Defines the maximum error-free data rate $C$ that can be transmitted over a channel with bandwidth $B$ and signal-to-noise ratio $S/N$.

$$ C = B \log_2 \left( 1 + \frac{S}{N} \right) \text{ bps} $$

As bandwidth $B \to \infty$, capacity limits to $C_\infty = 1.44 \frac{S}{N_0}$, where $N_0$ is noise power spectral density.

## 💡 7. Multiplexing
Combining multiple signals into one for transmission over a shared medium.
- **FDM (Frequency Division Multiplexing)**: Different frequency bands.
- **TDM (Time Division Multiplexing)**: Different time slots (common in PCM).
- **WDM (Wavelength Division Multiplexing)**: Optical fiber equivalent of FDM.

## 8. Random Signals, White Noise, and Thermal Noise
- **Random Process**: A collection of time functions (ensemble) defined by probabilistic models.
- **Thermal Noise**: Generated by random thermal motion of electrons. Noise power $P_n = k T B$, where $k$ is Boltzmann's constant, $T$ is temperature, $B$ is bandwidth.
- **White Noise**: Noise whose power spectral density is uniform over all frequencies: $S_n(f) = \frac{N_0}{2}$.

## 9. Line Codes and Pulse Shaping
Line coding maps digital data into electrical waveforms for baseband transmission.
- **Unipolar NRZ**: 1 is $+V$, 0 is $0$.
- **Polar NRZ**: 1 is $+V$, 0 is $-V$.
- **Manchester**: 1 is positive-to-negative transition, 0 is negative-to-positive transition. Self-clocking.
- **AMI (Alternate Mark Inversion)**: 0 is $0V$, 1 alternates between $+V$ and $-V$. No DC component.

**Pulse Shaping**: Used to minimize Inter-Symbol Interference (ISI).
Nyquist criterion for zero ISI: Raised Cosine Filter.

## 10. Error Control Coding
Adding redundancy to detect or correct errors.
- **Parity Check**: Adds 1 bit to make total number of 1s even or odd.
- **Block Codes**: Maps $k$ bits to $n$ bits ($n > k$). e.g., Hamming Codes.
- **Convolutional Codes**: Generates parity bits continuously using shift registers.

## 💡 Common Mistakes / Exam Tips

> [!WARNING]
> Confusing data rate with baud rate. Data rate (bps) = Baud rate (symbols/s) $\times$ bits per symbol.

> [!TIP]
> Memorize the PCM bandwidth formula: $BW \geq \frac{1}{2} R_b$, where $R_b = n \cdot f_s$.

## 📝 Quick Reference / Formula Summary

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **Nyquist Rate**: $f_s \geq 2 f_{max}$
> - **Quantization Noise Power**: $\frac{\Delta^2}{12}$
> - **Entropy**: $H(X) = - \sum p_i \log_2 p_i$
> - **Shannon Capacity**: $C = B \log_2(1 + S/N)$

## ✏️ Practice Problems
1. **Problem**: A continuous-time signal $x(t) = 10 \sin(1000\pi t)$ is sampled at Nyquist rate. Calculate the sampling frequency.
   **Answer Sketch**: Maximum frequency $f_m = 1000\pi / 2\pi = 500$ Hz. Nyquist rate $f_s = 2 f_m = 1000$ Hz.
2. **Problem**: A telephone channel has $B = 3$ kHz and $S/N = 30$ dB. Calculate channel capacity.
   **Answer Sketch**: $S/N_{linear} = 10^{30/10} = 1000$. $C = 3000 \log_2(1 + 1000) \approx 3000 \times 9.96 = 29.8$ kbps.
